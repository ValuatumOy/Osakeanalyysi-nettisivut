Core investment analysis

How the company creates economic value

Tesla combines a current automotive and energy platform with two pre-revenue options. Automotive supplies the installed fleet, manufacturing system and customer relationship through which FSD, charging and Robotaxi can scale; Energy Storage adds a separate hardware profit engine. Yet the earnings split and value split are materially different. FY2025 revenue is USD 94,827m, including USD 78,000m from Core Automotive, or 82.3% of the group total, while Robotaxi and Optimus together represent 86.6% of current-equivalent division EV. Core Automotive's 2028 operating requirement is 2.969m vehicles, approximately USD 42,100 revenue per vehicle and an 11.5% EBIT margin. Energy Storage requires USD 22.3bn of 2028 revenue and USD 3.345bn of EBITDA, while FSD requires USD 5.8bn of revenue and USD 2.610bn of EBITDA. The option businesses demand a different proof standard: Robotaxi needs permission, paid miles, utilisation and contribution margin; Optimus needs external productive hours, reliability, customer payback and manufacturing capacity. Texas authorisation, paid Cybercab service and more than 1m unsupervised miles move Robotaxi beyond demonstration, but 420 Texas registrations remain far below the approximately 1.11m vehicles required by the neutral case. Optimus has no public customer sales, making external deployment the harder gate. The BUY therefore rests on established operations remaining auditable while the options cross observable commercial thresholds.

Putting the divisions back together

The bridge values established businesses on revised 2028 operating metrics and values Robotaxi and Optimus through explicit probability trees. Established divisions contribute USD 260.5bn of present EV, while Robotaxi and Optimus contribute USD 1.672tn. Against the quoted USD 1,410.5bn EV, roughly USD 1.159tn is assigned to the two options after the locked bridge, making execution evidence the central valuation control.

Scenarios and verdict

The Bear case produces 66.81 USD per share and -81.2% because established businesses face slower automotive growth, persistent price pressure and energy normalisation while Robotaxi and Optimus receive no option value. It requires the existing earnings engines to weaken and the two largest value pools to fail permission, safe utilisation or useful autonomous work. The Base case produces 483.80 USD per share and +36.4%. It requires 2028 automotive capacity to support 2.969m vehicles, margin recovery, FSD subscriptions to convert into recognised recurring revenue, storage deployments to approach the operating build, and the probability-weighted option trees to remain credible. The Bull case produces 882.32 USD per share and +148.7%. It requires thousands of active driverless vehicles during 2027, federal compliance without material Cybercab redesign, millions of high-utilisation vehicles, externally verified Optimus productivity, multi-plant capacity and lower unit cost without sacrificing reliability. The earliest indicators are active vehicles, paid miles and contribution margin for Robotaxi, and external Optimus uptime, interventions, throughput and payback. The central debate is whether Tesla's manufacturing platform can turn regulated autonomy and general-purpose robotics into large, reliable businesses before the current valuation demands it. The evidence supports BUY because commercial Robotaxi evidence now exists and established operations provide a measurable floor, but the return compensates investors for concentrated option risk.

Risks & catalysts

Downside risks

  • Cybercab fails federal compliance audit (Unsupervised Autonomy and Robotaxi Network): AQ26002 requests for hardware or control changes would delay scale and raise vehicle cost, with audit requests as the early warning indicator - HIGH impact and structural, and a thesis-breaker if material redesign is required.
  • Driverless utilisation remains too low (Core Automotive): fleet growth without paid-mile growth would require more vehicles and capex per paid mile, with fleet growth faster than paid miles as the early warning - HIGH impact and thesis-breaker if utilisation does not scale.
  • Optimus requires intensive supervision (Optimus General-Purpose Robotics): human labour would return to cost per task, with no intervention or uptime disclosure as the early warning - HIGH impact and thesis-breaker if productive customer economics are not verified.
  • Automotive price cuts absorb cost savings (Supervised FSD and Connected Software): volume growth would fail to restore EBIT margin, with revenue per vehicle and gross margin decline as the early warning - HIGH impact and manageable.
  • Energy warranty charges recur (Energy Storage): underlying project margin and reputation would deteriorate, with further vendor-cell warranty true-ups as the early warning - MEDIUM impact and manageable.

Upside catalysts

  • NHTSA closes AQ26002 without material Cybercab redesign (near-term, Unsupervised Autonomy and Robotaxi Network): audit closure and favourable FMVSS treatment would raise probability and protect the unit-cost thesis.
  • Austin service moves to continuous 24/7 availability (near-term, Unsupervised Autonomy and Robotaxi Network): operating hours, fleet and paid miles would test utilisation and operational reliability.
  • Semi rollout and vehicle-capacity update (near-term, Core Automotive): named capacity and production cadence would support or challenge the 2028 volume build.
  • FSD subscriber and churn disclosure (medium-term, Supervised FSD and Connected Software): users, realisation and retention would validate recurring software economics.
  • External Optimus customer reports productive operation (medium-term, Optimus General-Purpose Robotics): uptime, interventions and task throughput would provide the major probability-validation event.
