Why the price is below peers

Both businesses are priced below their listed peer ranges for business-specific reasons rather than because the whole communication-equipment market faces one common problem. Network infrastructure carries 17.8x 2028 operating profit, below a 25.9x peer median and even below the lowest named peer multiple of 18.4x. Mobile infrastructure carries 10.4x, below a 14.4x peer median and the lowest named peer at 10.6x. Nokia’s radio unit produced a 2.8% operating margin in 2025, versus 19.7% at Ericsson, leaving little protection if equipment prices, volumes or product mix weaken. The mobile market outside China and Russia is forecast to remain flat to 2028, and five suppliers held 96% of the global market in 2025. The lower multiples therefore express doubts about Nokia’s margin durability, integration execution and competitive position; evidence of sustained margin improvement would change what the share price is paying for.

The first tests are operational and arrive in the next reporting periods. Network infrastructure sales growth should be compared with Nokia’s 12% to 14% 2026 constant-currency and portfolio guidance; below 12% would weaken the path towards the split’s EUR 9bn of 2028 sales, while delivery would support it. The annual outside-sales margin should reach 14% in the split; remaining at 13% would preserve the peer discount. Ericsson’s results provide the closest benchmark, including its SEK 38.6bn 2025 operating profit and 8.2x market multiple. Enterprise Campus Edge disposal, restructuring charges and the 35% to 55% free-cash-flow conversion target are additional execution indicators.
