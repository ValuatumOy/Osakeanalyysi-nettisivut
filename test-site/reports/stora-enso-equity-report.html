What the price pays for

The €10.0 share price is primarily a forest valuation rather than a mills valuation. Forests contribute €8.45 per share, based on €6.7bn or 79% of the €8.5bn reported forest value. The four mills contribute €6.05 per share before debt and other claims: Paperboard contributes €2.91 at 7.4x forecast 2028 operating profit of EUR 310m, Biomaterials €1.57 at 7.7x EUR 160m, Packaging €0.85 at 11.4x EUR 58m and Wood products €0.75 at 8.7x EUR 69m. Those components add to €14.5 per share, after which forecast debt and other claims, less cash, remove €4.53 per share. Forests are judged against reported asset value because much of their wood is sold internally: internal sales represented 62% of fourth-quarter 2025 Forest segment net sales. The 79% reference sits between Holmen’s 75% market reading and Handelsbanken’s 80% assessment of SCA, while Stora Enso’s 2025 sale of one forest block occurred at 99.2% of reported value. The composition means the share price is most sensitive to how the forest portfolio is separated and priced, with mill earnings providing the smaller and more cyclical component.

Why the price is below peers

The four mill businesses are priced at about 8x 2028 operating profit compared with about 9x for listed peers, but the gap reflects business-specific execution risks rather than one uniform industry discount. Paperboard is priced at 7.4x against an 8.3x peer median despite a forecast margin recovery from 2.9% in 2025 to 7% in 2028. European cartonboard overcapacity, weaker second-quarter 2026 operating rates and the Oulu line’s 750,000-tonne ramp-up make that recovery unproven. Biomaterials trades at 7.7x against an 8.4x industry median while its margin is expected to rise from 9.6% to 13%; the Skutskär product change and the distinction between total-sales and outside-sales margins add uncertainty. Packaging is at 11.4x against an 11.5x median, but its 5% forecast margin is well above the 1.4% 2025 margin after oversupply limited price pass-through. Wood products is at 8.7x against a 9.5x median after a 2025 margin of -0.1%, with high log costs, Veitsiluoto’s closure and weaker reporting visibility. The price therefore gives partial, not full, credit for forecast margin recovery.

What to watch

The Swedish forest separation is the first major event. Completion and listing are expected in the first half of 2027, subject to General Meeting approval, and a traded price would replace today’s indirect comparisons with a market percentage of reported forest value. A reading near Holmen’s 75% would lower the forest reference, while a reading near the 99.2% 2025 transaction would raise it. For Paperboard, Oulu’s first production disclosure and progress toward its 750,000-tonne annual capacity will test the 7% 2028 margin against 2.9% in 2025; the second-quarter 2026 consumer-board margin was 6.5%. Biomaterials requires outside-sales margins near 13% by 2028, while the Skutskär shutdown and EUR 19m fluff-pulp investment will show whether the new product mix offsets lost softwood volume. Packaging needs the Integrated Packaging margin to sustain or exceed 4.9%, versus 1.4% in 2025, and price increases must begin to overcome oversupply. Wood products requires closures at Veitsiluoto and Gruvön to improve the remaining operations while log costs ease. Quarterly results against the 2026 forecast will show whether these conditions are emerging.
