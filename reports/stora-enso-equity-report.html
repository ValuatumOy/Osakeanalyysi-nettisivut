The evidence also separates reported asset value from profit multiples: forests are read against published asset readings, while the mills are read against 2028 operating profit. The interpretation of the share price depends first on how investors value the forests and then on whether the mills deliver the margins embedded in the forecast.

Why the price is below peers

The four mill businesses are priced at about 9x 2028 operating profit, compared with listed peers at about 10x, because their forecasts require recoveries that have not yet been demonstrated. Packaging materials must move from a 2.9% 2025 margin to 7% in 2028, with Oulu’s 750,000 tonnes of annual capacity targeted to run during 2027. The market has evidence for caution: Metsä Board reported a −9.5% 2025 operating margin and MM Board & Paper −3.65%. Biomaterials must rise from a 9.6% 2025 margin to 13% while pulp demand and prices were weak in Europe and China. Wood products must recover from a −0.1% margin while the Central European sawmill and building-solutions review remains unresolved and Veitsiluoto is closing. Packaging solutions must move from 1.4% to 5% despite excess capacity, with the German corrugated operations sold in July 2026. These are business-specific reasons for the discount rather than a simple industry-wide comparison. The peer evidence is also not uniform: Packaging materials is compared with three listed peers, while Biomaterials and Wood products use industry quartiles because fewer than three peers could be read. The share-price composition therefore reflects operational uncertainty, capacity changes and forecast margin expansion rather than a single group-level multiple.

The first structural event is the Swedish forest separation, planned for the first half of 2027 and subject to shareholder approval. Approval and a listing would give the largest part of the businesses a daily market price, testing the company’s reported forest value of EUR 5.8bn for the planned Swedish company and the 75% reading used here. Without approval, the forests remain inside the group and continue to be interpreted through broker readings ranging from 68% to 82%, which cover different holdings. The first operating test is Oulu: quarterly delivery volumes should move towards 750,000 tonnes during 2027, while Packaging materials’ outside-sales margin should progress from 2.9% in 2025 towards 7% in 2028. Biomaterials requires pulp prices, demand and mix to lift the margin from 9.6% towards 13%, with Skutskär’s closure and fluff-pulp investment providing a changed base. Wood products needs a result from the Central European review, fuller mills after Veitsiluoto and an improvement from a −0.1% margin towards 4%. Packaging solutions needs evidence that capacity reduction and customer-mix actions can lift the margin from 1.4% towards 5%. These events determine whether the share price remains dominated by asset value or reflects stronger mill earnings.
