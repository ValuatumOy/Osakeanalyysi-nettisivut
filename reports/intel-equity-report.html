Executive summary

Intel Corporation (INTC, NASDAQ Global Select) is a US semiconductor designer and manufacturer whose valuation now rests on a sovereign-backed foundry turnaround; in this Valuatum equity research report dated 5 June 2026 we rate the stock SELL with a 12-month target price of 98.00 USD against a current price of 114.68 USD, implying roughly 14.5% downside. The core valuation tension is a $500 billion premium over fundamentals. Intel's $646.5 billion enterprise value embeds a premium over the roughly $80 billion valuation implied by its $5.2 billion projected 2026 consolidated operating profit. The product divisions, Client Computing and Data Center and AI, generate a combined $16.4 billion in operating profit, but at the group level that is severely diluted by the Intel Foundry, which currently burns approximately $9.6 billion annually while consuming $24.3 billion in gross capital expenditure.

With the absolute re-rating driver being commercial validation of the 18A node, we conclude the premium is unsupported and reiterate the SELL call.

Core investment analysis

How the company creates economic value

The foundational premise of the current valuation is that Intel's historical product businesses must fund the capital-intensive incubation of a sovereign-backed commercial foundry. Intel earns its economic output almost entirely from its product design divisions: the Client Computing Group functions as a mature cash engine generating the bulk of consolidated volume and operating profit, while the Data Center and AI segment supplies margin expansion and the growth narrative. Combined they produce an estimated $16.4 billion in annualized operating profit, but at the consolidated level that profit is severely diluted by the Intel Foundry, which burns approximately $9.6 billion annually, while DCAI alone commands 46.4% of the $646.5 billion enterprise value.

Cross-pool bridge

The mechanical driver of Intel's forecast is the intersection of product profitability and manufacturing capital intensity. The product divisions support $52.2 billion of net sales and $16.4 billion of operating profit, but after deducting the $9.6 billion foundry loss and $1.5 billion of corporate overhead the group is left with only $5.2 billion of consolidated EBIT for 2026. The company is projected to generate $24.4 billion in EBITDA but must immediately deploy $24.3 billion in gross capital expenditure on foundry infrastructure, so a 15x to 20x EBIT framework supports an enterprise value of only $80 billion to $100 billion versus a market value of $646.5 billion, a premium of over $500 billion based entirely on future optionality.

Scenarios and verdict

Scenarios differ fundamentally based on Intel Foundry execution and the competitive resilience of the Data Center segment, with external customer adoption of the 18A node causing the largest valuation swing. In the downside case, poor 18A defect densities or delays keep tier-1 designers locked into TSMC while AMD takes x86 server share, dragging equity value to roughly $255 billion. The upside depends on breaking TSMC's monopoly via massive 18A volume commitments, which paired with strong Gaudi 3 adoption could expand enterprise value to $850 billion. The biggest unsupported assumption is that external commercial demand for foundry services will materialize at a scale large enough to absorb $24 billion in annual capital expenditure.

Risks & catalysts

Downside risks

  • Capital Exhaustion (Group / Equity): capex requirements outpace CCG and DCAI cash generation, with consecutive quarters of worsening free cash flow as the early warning — HIGH impact and a thesis-breaker if combined with competitive pressure.
  • TSMC Pricing Retaliation (Intel Foundry): TSMC lowers wafer prices to starve the 18A node of volume, with TSMC wafer discount announcements as the early warning — HIGH impact, structural.
  • ARM PC Architecture Shift (Client Computing Group): Qualcomm and Apple erode x86 laptop dominance, with ARM-based laptop market-share gains as the early warning — MEDIUM impact, structural.
  • AI Compute Commoditization (Data Center and AI): inference pricing drops and compresses Gaudi 3 margins, with AI hardware margin compression as the early warning — MEDIUM impact, manageable.

Upside catalysts

  • 18A External Customer Wins (near-term, Intel Foundry): announced tier-1 fabless volume contracts validate the foundry's $170B option value and are the hardest evidence the turnaround is commercially viable.
  • Data Center Market Share (near-term, Data Center and AI): Xeon 6 versus AMD EPYC results defend DCAI margin, revenue share and the $300B EV, and are the easiest catalyst to observe quarterly.
  • AI Accelerator Adoption (near-term, Data Center and AI): the Gaudi 3 revenue run-rate determines the sustainability of the AI premium.
  • Foundry Operating Margin (medium-term, Intel Foundry): a reduction in the -$9.6B operating loss measures absorption of fixed capex.
