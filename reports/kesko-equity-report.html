Core investment analysis

Business Model Quality

Kesko operates as a highly diversified Northern European retail and wholesale conglomerate, but its enterprise value is heavily concentrated in defensive domestic grocery operations. It generates revenue across three pools: Grocery Trade (roughly half of group sales), Building and Technical Trade (nearly 38% of sales via a heavy B2B Nordic footprint) and the smaller Car Trade division.

Earnings Quality and EV Allocation

Because reported EBIT is materially depressed by one-off and non-cash items, it cannot be used mechanically for valuation. The analysis instead relies on gross comparable segment EBIT of €680 million as the true proxy for recurring operating economics, allocated across the €11.67 billion group enterprise value.

Bridging from €680m in normalized EBIT to a projected €703m relies entirely on incremental BTT volume throughput as the grocery segment plateaus. A €750 million swing to positive €501 million FCF assumes heavy acquisition outlays are complete and working capital tied up in slow-moving BTT inventory is released as sales velocity increases.

Scenario Logic and Verdict

If BTT margins stabilize at 4.5% rather than 6.5%, the segment supports only ~€2.5 billion in EV, blowing a €1.2 billion hole in group EV and eroding over 14% of equity value through leverage. The valuation is defensible on current terms provided the macro environment normalizes, pricing a return to historical mean construction margins backstopped by grocery cash flows.
