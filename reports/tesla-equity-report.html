What the price pays for

The split assigns 21 USD per share to the businesses already generating substantial sales and 383 USD to Autonomy and Optimus. Cars contributes 17 USD at 8.1x forecast 2028 operating profit of USD 8.1bn, while Energy contributes 5 USD at 14.5x forecast 2028 operating profit of USD 1.3bn. A deduction of 3 USD per share covers debt and other claims, less cash. The remaining 307 USD assigned to Autonomy requires USD 1.35tn of annual 2040 sales and about USD 337bn of operating profit at a 25% margin. Optimus contributes 77 USD and requires USD 449bn of 2040 sales and about USD 94bn of operating profit at a 21% margin. Those requirements dwarf current activity: Tesla's whole-company sales were USD 94,827m in 2025, while Autonomy had no separately reported sales and Optimus was at the first factory stage. The price is therefore made predominantly from successful scaling of new businesses rather than from near-term Cars and Energy earnings. The decisive question is whether future operating evidence can replace the present gap between USD 9.3bn of 2028 whole-company operating profit and USD 451bn required in 2040.

Why the price differs from peers

The whole company trades at 171x forecast 2028 EV/EBIT and 562x forecast 2026 EV/EBIT, whereas the peer median is 13.2x 2027 EV/EBITDA and 18.8x 2027 P/E. The difference is not explained by Cars or Energy alone. Cars is assigned 8.1x 2028 operating profit against an industry median of 7.1x, with General Motors at 7.5x and BYD at 8.7x. Energy is assigned 14.5x 2028 operating profit, exactly the solar-peer median, between 11.4x and 17.8x. These established businesses account for only 21 USD per share before claims. The unusual whole-company multiple reflects the 383 USD per share assigned to Autonomy and Optimus, where current sales are negligible but the required 2040 sales are USD 1.35tn and USD 449bn respectively. The comparison is also sensitive to profit margins: Cars requires 6% in 2028 against a latest-quarter company margin of 2.6%, while Energy also requires 6% operating margin. Peer figures are not a clean operating comparison: Rivian, Mobileye and Lyft negative multiples are excluded as outliers, and Enphase's 21x multiple uses past operating profit rather than Energy's 2028 forecast. The price therefore embeds a future-business premium, not simply a conventional automotive or solar multiple.

What to watch

The next evidence is measurable. Autonomy needs a separately disclosed annual sales figure, because the current disclosure reports nearly 2 million cumulative unsupervised miles rather than revenue. Sales of USD 58.9bn are required by 2030, followed by USD 550bn in 2035 and USD 1.35tn in 2040. Cost per paid mile is equally important: Tesla's 0.30 USD goal had no deadline and had not been met by 21 October 2026, while Morgan Stanley estimated fully autonomous Tesla cost at 0.74 USD per mile excluding safety monitors. Permission to run without a safety driver in California and other large states determines whether six-city service can become a national fleet. Optimus needs outside deliveries around the end of 2027, weekly output moving towards 20,000 robots and a customer price with unit cost below it; the first line's one-million-robot design capacity is not production. Cars needs deliveries, automotive revenue and operating margin to move towards the 6% split assumption, while Energy needs storage volume, order flow and Texas Megapack utilisation. A SpaceX combination could also change which businesses shareholders own, but Tesla's committee had agreed no terms as of 21 October 2026. These milestones determine whether the price remains concentrated in unproven future operations or gains support from reported commercial scale.

Risks & what to watch

Risks

  • Autonomy commercial scale: sales may remain immaterial while the price requires USD 58.9bn by 2030 and USD 1.35tn by 2040, with separate Autonomy revenue not yet disclosed; early warning is the absence of an annual sales figure.
  • Autonomy cost and permission: the 0.30 USD per paid-mile goal has no deadline and California listed Tesla Robotaxi LLC for testing with a safety driver, limiting fleet deployment; early warning is cost above 0.74 USD per mile or no driverless approvals.
  • Optimus adoption: outside deliveries planned around the end of 2027 may not materialise, leaving robots inside Tesla plants without proof that customers will pay; early warning is no external delivery by the planned date.
  • Cars margin and volume: the 6% 2028 operating-margin assumption is above the latest reported 2.6% and third-quarter deliveries fell 2% to 486,532; early warning is weak deliveries alongside automotive revenue below USD 20,560m.
  • Energy utilisation: factory capacity may not translate into output if equipment uptime, components or demand constrain production; early warning is storage revenue below USD 2,881m or cost of revenue above USD 1,927m in a quarter.

What to watch

  • Driverless approval and annual Autonomy sales disclosure (near-term, Autonomy): a separate sales figure and permission without safety drivers would demonstrate progress towards USD 58.9bn of 2030 sales.
  • Paid-mile cost reduction (near-term, Autonomy): a reported cost moving towards 0.30 USD per mile would support the 25% 2040 operating-margin path.
  • External Optimus deliveries (medium-term, Optimus): sales to other companies around the end of 2027 would show that the product works beyond Tesla's own facilities.
  • Optimus production ramp (medium-term, Optimus): output approaching 20,000 robots a week would show that the first line can move towards its one-million-robot annual design capacity.
  • Higher factory utilisation (near-term, Cars and Energy): improving vehicle deliveries, a Cars margin approaching 6%, and sustained Texas Megapack output would make the established-business forecasts more attainable.
