Investment thesis — three reasons

01 Robotaxi Scale 3.0m fleet

Robotaxi must expand from tens to low hundreds of unsupervised vehicles to approximately 3.0 million by 2029, generating revenue of USD 90.0bn and EBIT of USD 31.5bn. Waymo already operates over 3,000 vehicles and serves 500,000 paid rides weekly, making Tesla’s required ramp the decisive execution risk behind a 62.3% EV allocation.

02 Optimus Volume Ramp 3.3m units

Optimus valuation requires approximately 3.3 million units sold annually by 2029 at an average selling price near USD 30,000, producing revenue of USD 99.0bn and EBIT of USD 14.9bn. That volume is roughly six times the 542,000 industrial robots installed globally in 2024, while Tesla remains at internal pilots and installing Gen 3 lines.

03 Automotive Margin 4.6% margin

Automotive EBIT margin has fallen from 16.8% in 2022 to 4.6% in 2025, despite FY2025A revenue of USD 82,056m and gross profit of USD 13,292m. The 2029 case assumes core EBIT of USD 7,200m valued at 12x, leaving group value dependent on option businesses rather than durable car-business profitability.

Thesis breaker: An unsupervised robotaxi fleet that remains legally or operationally small through 2028 would eliminate the 62% EV allocation to Robotaxi and drive valuation towards the bear case.

Scenarios and verdict

The Bear outcome of 49.06 USD per share and -85.1% downside requires a regulatory stop, a safety event or a 2027 fleet still measured in hundreds, leaving the cash businesses valued as a manufacturer and the option EBIT removed. The Base outcome of 490.81 USD per share and +49.4% requires multi-city paid unsupervised Robotaxi operations by 2027, manufacturing capacity in the hundreds of thousands by 2028, and meaningful third-party Optimus shipments in 2027–2028. The Bull outcome of 753.70 USD per share and +129.4% requires those volumes plus higher margins or a market willing to pay more than 30x. The earliest indicators are a California CPUC driverless paid-deployment permit, third-party fleet counts above the low thousands, Optimus shipments and ASP disclosure, and FY2028 group EBIT above USD 9,900m consensus. More geofenced territory without more vehicles would not prove the Base case. The central debate is whether Tesla can turn a vertically integrated vehicle and vision-only platform into a 3.0 million-vehicle Robotaxi network before regulatory, competitive and capital constraints intervene, or whether the USD 1,296,000m EV is capitalising two pre-revenue programmes ahead of evidence. The balance of evidence favours SELL.

Risks & catalysts

Downside risks

  • Unsupervised approval delay (Automotive): NHTSA action or a California permit block could prevent the fleet expanding beyond hundreds, with NHTSA action and CPUC permit status as the early warning - HIGH impact and a thesis-breaker if it occurs.
  • AV competition splits the fare pool (Robotaxi / Autonomous Mobility Network): Waymo, Apollo Go and Uber partnerships could fragment the USD 400,000m addressable market and make USD 90,000m of revenue unreachable, with competitor city expansions as the early warning - HIGH impact and structural.
  • Humanoid reliability and cost miss (Optimus / Humanoid Robotics): failure to reach a USD 25,500 cost or reliable household performance would destroy the 15.0% EBIT thesis, with internal test failures and high warranty as the early warning - HIGH impact and structural.
  • Cell and capex collision (Optimus / Humanoid Robotics): simultaneous factory scale-ups could exhaust the balance sheet as 2029 free cash flow to firm reaches negative USD 14,500m, with delayed Texas Giga timelines as the early warning - MEDIUM impact and manageable.
  • Vehicle price war and fading EV credits (Automotive): BYD pricing and lost incentives could trap core Automotive EBIT at a 4.6% margin, with sequential MSRP cuts as the early warning - MEDIUM impact and structural.

Upside catalysts

  • California CPUC driverless paid-deployment permit (near-term, Robotaxi / Autonomous Mobility Network): approval would unlock the largest US fare pool and distinguish a Texas-and-Florida pilot from national deployment.
  • TCMV / EU FSD recognition vote (near-term, Robotaxi / Autonomous Mobility Network): the October 2026 committee outcome could unlock EU supervised scale, although it would not by itself prove Robotaxi EBIT.
  • Optimus third-party shipments and ASP disclosure (medium-term, Optimus / Humanoid Robotics): volume targeted by summer 2027 would convert design capacity into demonstrated real selling prices.
  • Group EBIT exceeding USD 9,900m consensus (long-term, Automotive): FY2028 earnings above consensus would validate option EBIT scaling in reported results.
  • Capex trajectory against free cash flow to firm (long-term, Robotaxi / Autonomous Mobility Network): a USD 75,000m capex print alongside negative USD 14,500m free cash flow to firm would test the balance sheet against the volume ramp.
