Core investment analysis

How the company creates economic value

Tesla generates almost all revenue from two physical-asset businesses: Automotive, at 86.5% of FY2025 external sales, and Energy Generation & Storage, at 13.5%. Automotive remains the main source of absolute gross profit, but reported EBIT margins fell from 16.8% in 2022 to 4.6% by the end of 2025, while Energy is scaling rapidly at a materially higher 29.8% gross margin and contributes 22.2% of group gross profit. Under a normalized SOTP framework, approximately 77.6% of the USD 1,573,233 million enterprise value is instead assigned to FSD Software & Network Services, Robotaxi and Optimus — three divisions that generate negligible distinct external revenue and remain cash-consuming research and pilot programs. Tesla's group EV/EBITDA multiple of 85.15x for 2026 cannot be explained by traditional auto or industrial multiples; the key question is whether unit economics can generate the tens of billions of recurring EBIT the option premium requires.

Cross-division bridge

The 2026 forecast is a transition year: revenue grows 7.9% to USD 102,329 million, entirely supported by stabilizing Automotive deliveries and continued Energy Generation & Storage growth, while EBITDA rises 75.9% to USD 18,475 million as margin pressure stops and reverses to a 5.6% EBIT margin despite a USD 12,729 million depreciation burden. The divisions holding 78% of enterprise value — FSD, Robotaxi and Optimus — do not drive this earnings forecast; instead they drive cash burn, with free cash flow falling to negative USD 1,741 million on USD 19,488 million of gross capex funding AI compute infrastructure and future Robotaxi depots. At a standard 15x-20x industrial or auto multiple, Tesla's USD 5,746 million of 2026 recurring EBIT supports less than USD 115 billion of enterprise value; the remaining USD 1.22 trillion requires Autonomy and Robotics to eventually generate roughly USD 48 billion of new recurring annual EBIT at a 25x multiple.

Scenarios and verdict

The bear scenario shows what breaks the thesis: if regulators conclude vision-only hardware is inadequate for unsupervised autonomy, the USD 1.22 trillion option premium disappears and Tesla is valued on Automotive and Energy alone, producing an EV of USD 450,000 million, equity of USD 458,137 million and a share price of USD 121.98 (-62.5%). The base scenario requires execution on the current roadmap — commercial robotaxis under Texas SB 2807, an EU-wide FSD rollout building on the Netherlands provisional approval, and continued Megapack scaling — implying USD 420.65 (+29.4%). The bull scenario assumes monopoly-like adoption of the Robotaxi network and generalized L5 autonomy, implying USD 667.15 (+105.3%). FSD Software and Robotaxi together represent approximately 63.5% of enterprise value: the debate over Tesla is no longer about EV adoption or manufacturing efficiency, but the probability of solving generalized AI for physical applications.

  • Vision-only deemed unsafe for L5 (FSD Software & Network Services): regulators mandate LiDAR or radar redundancy for unsupervised operation, with NHTSA or EU regulatory blockage as the early warning - HIGH impact and a thesis-breaker if it occurs.
  • Chinese OEMs achieve parity in ADAS & robotics (Automotive (Core)): BYD and XPeng commoditize EV hardware and humanoid robotics, with market share loss in China / EU as the early warning - HIGH impact, structural.
  • Capital burn (Robotaxi / Autonomous Mobility): Dojo and fleet capex drain cash flow before the network scales, with free cash flow staying structurally negative as the early warning - MEDIUM impact, manageable.
  • Margin degradation (Automotive (Core)): the auto price war forces margins structurally below 5%, with additional vehicle price cuts as the early warning - MEDIUM impact, manageable.

Upside catalysts

  • Texas SB 2807 commercial robotaxi launch (near-term, Robotaxi / Autonomous Mobility): paid autonomous miles logged and cost per mile validate L4 commercial unit economics; the most observable near-term catalyst.
  • EU Commission FSD vote (near-term, FSD Software & Network Services): conversion of the Netherlands provisional type approval to a full EU approval unlocks the European software TAM.
  • Auto gross margin stabilization (near-term, Automotive (Core)): margin excluding regulatory credits secures the cash flow that funds Autonomy and Robotics R&D burn.
  • Optimus Gen-3 mass production (medium-term, Optimus / Humanoid Robotics): production rate versus XPeng's 1,000-unit/month target validates physical manufacturing scaling.
  • Energy Megapack deployments (long-term, Energy Generation & Storage): the division's gross profit share of the group provides a high-margin earnings floor.
