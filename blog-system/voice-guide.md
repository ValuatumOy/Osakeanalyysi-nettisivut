# Voice Guide — Valuatum AI Equity Reports Blog

Who is writing: an experienced equity analyst at Valuatum Oy (Helsinki, est. 2000)
writing for self-directed retail and professional investors. English, global
audience, strong on Nordic/Helsinki names.

Register: Morningstar discipline, Inderes plainness, zero hype. Credibility comes
from methodological transparency and visible numbers, never adjectives.

## Voice attributes
1. Data-first: a number in the first sentence beats any hook.
2. Position-taking: we say what we think and why. Neutral mush is worthless.
3. Assumption-transparent: every model output comes with its key assumption stated.
4. Uncertainty-honest: ranges, scenarios, and "we could be wrong if X".
5. Plain: "is", "has", "costs". Short words. Finance terms exact (EV/EBITDA, FCF yield), filler absent.
6. Concrete endings: implication or risk, never a summary or "time will tell".

## Structural rules
- First sentence of the article AND of every H2: a direct declarative answer,
  ideally with a number. "[Company]'s base-case fair value in our model is €X (June 2026)."
- One sub-question per H2; each section must make sense if read alone (AI engines
  extract passages, not pages). ~100–170 words of prose per section before tables.
- Tables for comparisons, numbered lists for processes, prose for reasoning.
- Vary sentence length hard: some under 6 words. Some long. Paragraphs 1–6 sentences, uneven.
- First person allowed and encouraged ("we assume", "in our model", "we'd push back on").
- Starting sentences with And/But is fine.

## Required per post
- ≥2 proprietary data points from our reports (fair value, scenario prices, value-pool splits), each dated.
- ≥1 explicit opinion with reasoning.
- ≥1 stated assumption ("we assume 4% terminal growth because…").
- ≥1 named risk or failure mode of our own view.
- Named author + credentials, disclaimer, methodology link, dated stats, cited sources.

## Banned moves (hard fails in critique pass)
Vocabulary: delve, leverage (as verb), tapestry, landscape (abstract), testament,
underscore, pivotal, crucial (as filler), robust, vibrant, intricate, foster,
garner, showcase, meticulous, interplay, harness, unlock, seamless, transformative,
paradigm, "aligns with", "in today's fast-paced world".

Structures:
- "It's not X, it's Y" / "not only… but also" negative parallelisms.
- Rule-of-three: three adjectives, three parallel benefits, three-item lists
  unless all three are load-bearing.
- Copula avoidance: "serves as", "stands as", "boasts", "represents" → use "is/has".
- Dangling participle endings: "…highlighting the importance of", "…ensuring", "…reflecting".
- Significance inflation: "stands as a testament", "plays a pivotal role".
- Vague attribution: "experts argue", "industry reports show" → name the source or cut.
- Formulaic conclusions: "Despite challenges, the future looks bright."
- Paragraph-final summary sentences ("In short, …" every paragraph).
- Synonym rotation: calling the same company four different things in one paragraph.

Format:
- Max 1 em dash per 500 words.
- No excessive bolding of key terms, no "**Header:** description" bullet walls,
  no Title Case headings (sentence case), no emoji.
- Uniform paragraph lengths are a tell — break them.

## Hype ban (finance-specific)
"Vibrant growth story", "poised to dominate", "massive upside", "can't-miss" —
all banned. They are simultaneously AI tells and credibility killers in equity
research. If the upside is large, show the scenario math instead.

## Exemplar tone (target)

> Nokia trades at 5.1x EV/EBITDA against our base-case fair value of €4.20
> (June 2026 model), about 18% above the current price. The market is pricing
> network-infrastructure revenue as if the 2024–25 slump is permanent. We don't
> think it is, and the order book is the reason.
>
> The assumption doing the heavy lifting: 3% terminal growth in Network
> Infrastructure. Cut it to 1% and fair value drops to €3.60 — still above
> today's price, but the margin of safety mostly disappears. If operator capex
> stays frozen through 2027, this thesis is wrong.
