Wärtsilä Stock Analysis: Why We Moved to Hold

Lauri Hynönen · 20 July 2026 · 7 min read Updated 31 July 2026

Is Wärtsilä a buy or sell right now?

In our 7 July 2026 model, Wärtsilä (WRT1V.HE) is a hold. We set a 12-month target of EUR 32.10 against a EUR 31.89 share price, about 0.7% of upside. The stock sits on top of our target, which is the definition of appropriately valued.

This is a change from our previous report, which rated the stock a sell at a EUR 30.00 target against a EUR 36.12 price. The gap closed the way we expected: the share price fell toward the target rather than the target rising to meet the price. Once that happened the sell case was spent. What remains is a business priced at 18.0x 2026e EV/EBIT, already below its 19.5x normalised historical average and the 20.2x peer median, which leaves little room for further re-rating in either direction. The full workings are in our Wärtsilä equity report.

The interesting question is no longer whether the multiple is too high. It is whether the growth premium inside it gets delivered. About 77% of the EUR 16.1 billion enterprise value we test is backed by existing, proven cash flows at a mature 15x EBIT baseline. The other EUR 3.7 billion is a bet on two specific things, and the rest of this piece is about them.

Where Wärtsilä's value actually sits

About 96.8% of Wärtsilä's EUR 16.5 billion enterprise value sits in just two businesses. In our 7 July 2026 model, Wärtsilä Marine carries EUR 7,847 million of that value (48.7%) and Wärtsilä Energy EUR 7,755 million (48.1%). Energy Storage is a EUR 195 million call option on the battery transition, and a small Portfolio unit under disposal accounts for the rest.

Together the two big pools carry 80.2% of group revenue and 92.6% of operating profit. Most stock pages quote a single P/E near 24x and stop there. The value-pool view shows where the risk actually lives.

Why the 18.0x multiple is the whole argument

Wärtsilä's enterprise value works out to 18.0 times forward EBIT in our 2026 model, on group EBIT bridging from EUR 826 million to EUR 918 million. That single multiple carries the argument. To justify it, Energy EBIT has to climb from EUR 362 million to about EUR 517 million, and Marine has to push its 12.4% EBIT margin toward the 14.7% to 14.9% benchmark Kongsberg already earns.

We approach this backwards. Rather than forecast a price, we ask what the current price already assumes, then judge whether those assumptions hold. That is what reverse valuation does. At 18.0x the market is no longer paying a peak multiple, which is why we are at hold rather than sell. But it is still paying for both of those improvements to arrive. Neither is delivered yet. This is the same process our AI research pipeline applies to every covered name, from Wärtsilä to Nokia.

The bull, base and bear cases

Our scenario bridge runs from EUR 22.67 per share in the bear case to EUR 36.34 in the bull case, with the base case at EUR 30.42, about 4.6% below the July price. Each scenario tests what EBITDA multiple the group can hold, then adds Wärtsilä's EUR 1.8 billion net cash to reach equity value.

Our 12-month target of EUR 32.10 sits above the base case, and that is deliberate. The bear path is a genuine 28.9% drawdown, but it needs the EBITDA margin to fall to 12.0% from a 17.2% base, and that is a deeper operational reversal than a simple multiple de-rating. The EUR 1.8 billion net cash pile is why the bear case is EUR 22.67 and not far lower. It is a real floor, worth about 10% of market capitalisation.

The data-centre order wave is the swing factor

Wärtsilä Energy has to convert 2.4 gigawatts of installed US data-centre capacity into a service annuity, lifting segment EBIT from EUR 362 million to the EUR 517 million its EUR 7.75 billion allocated enterprise value requires at 15x. That is the single largest swing factor in the valuation. The order book is real: Wärtsilä reported it at an all-time high of EUR 8.9 billion after a 10% rise in first-quarter order intake.

The question is durability, not size. Buyers themselves now describe behind-the-meter gas as a bridge to the grid rather than a permanent fix. Interconnection queues run for years, a dedicated gas plant goes up in about 18 months, and once the grid catches up the reason to keep ordering new engines weakens. If a large part of this is a one-time capacity scramble, the annuity never builds and the growth premium goes with it. That is the risk we watch most closely, and it cuts both ways from here.

What would make this call wrong

We are wrong in the bullish direction if two things hold at once: hyperscaler gas demand keeps compounding past 2028, and maritime decarbonisation forces a retrofit cycle that pushes Marine revenue toward EUR 3.7 billion by 2027 to 2029. In that world Marine EBIT reaches EUR 523 million, the growth premium is earned, and the bull case at EUR 36.34 is live.

The maritime leg is where the timing works against the bulls. The IMO's Net-Zero Framework, the rulebook meant to push shipowners toward cleaner fuels and engine retrofits, was approved in April 2025 but stalled at an extraordinary session that October. Its earliest entry into force has slipped from March 2027 to March 2028. The eventual demand is probably coming. It is not the imminent catalyst that a premium multiple assumes.

We are wrong in the bearish direction if the Energy annuity stalls and the multiple keeps compressing toward the bear case. At 18.0x, below both the historical norm and the peer median, we think the market has already taken out the froth. That is why this is a hold and not a call in either direction.
