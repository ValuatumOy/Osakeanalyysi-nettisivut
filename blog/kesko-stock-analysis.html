Kesko Stock Analysis: Fair Value and Dividend (2026)

Lauri Hynönen · 22 July 2026 · 6 min read

Is Kesko stock a buy in 2026?

Kesko (KESKOB.HE) rates BUY in our June 2026 equity model, with a base-case fair value of 25.92 EUR against the 20.60 EUR share price the report was set against, about 26% upside. The call rests on one judgment. The market is pricing Kesko's Building and Technical Trade division at its 3.8% trough margin, and we think that margin recovers as Nordic construction volumes normalise.

Kesko is not a growth story. It is one of the larger names on the Helsinki exchange, a Northern European retail and wholesale group whose 11.7 bn EUR enterprise value sits mostly in defensive Finnish grocery. The cheap part of the business is the cyclical part. Buy the construction recovery and grocery cash flow pays you to wait. That is the position we take.

We could be wrong. The reference price has drifted since the report, and a stalled construction cycle would leave the cyclical division cheap for longer. The scenario math below shows how much that assumption is worth. For the full model, see our Kesko equity report.

What is Kesko worth in our bear, base and bull cases?

Our reverse-valuation grid puts Kesko's fair value at 14.54 EUR in the bear case, 20.75 EUR in the base case and 25.34 EUR in the bull case (June 2026 model), with a separate 12-month fundamental target of 25.92 EUR. The spread is wide on purpose. Almost all of it comes from two variables: whether grocery holds a margin above 6%, and how fast Building and Technical Trade climbs back toward its 6% to 8% target.

We assume BTT comparable margins recover from the 3.8% trough toward 6.5%, and that grocery stays above 6%. That assumption is doing the heavy lifting. Cut BTT's stabilised margin to 4.5% and the segment supports roughly 1.2 bn EUR less enterprise value, which erodes more than 14% of equity value because of the leverage in the capital structure.

Where does Kesko make its money?

Grocery Trade generates 418m EUR of comparable EBIT, about 62% of Kesko's segment profit, on roughly a 33.5% share of the Finnish grocery market. That share is K Group's full-year 2025 figure, second to cooperative S-Group at around 48% and ahead of Lidl, per Kesko's own market-share release. Grocery is the anchor: a defensive, cash-generative business in one of Europe's most consolidated retail markets.

The other two segments carry the swing. Building and Technical Trade is priced not on its current 179m EUR EBIT but on a recovery from the trough. Car Trade is the smallest pool, valued on current cash flows with a lower multiple for auto cyclicality. Here is how our June 2026 model splits the group.

Grocery subsidises a depressed construction division. That is the tension the whole valuation turns on.

How safe is Kesko's dividend?

Kesko's dividend yields 4.9% in our 2026 model, on a payout we estimate near 87% of earnings. That is a high payout, and it leans on cash flow the model expects to improve rather than on a fat coverage cushion. Free cash flow swings to a positive 501m EUR in 2026 in our numbers, as slow-moving construction inventory clears, which is what funds both the dividend and the deleveraging.

The balance sheet is the thing to watch. Net debt of 3.38 bn EUR runs at 2.1x EBITDA in our 2026 estimate, inflated by IFRS 16 lease liabilities and consuming close to 29% of enterprise value. The dividend is not at risk in the base case. But it is not bulletproof either: a payout near 87% plus that leverage means the yield rests on the free cash flow inflection actually arriving.

Kesko itself guides to 650m to 750m EUR of comparable operating profit for 2026, in line with the recurring earnings our model relies on.

What is the main risk to our Kesko view?

The thesis breaks if a Finnish grocery price war permanently resets Kesko's grocery margin below 6%. That is the one risk to our BUY we take most seriously. If Lidl or S-Group forces a margin reset to grab footfall, the defensive earnings that underpin the whole valuation get repriced, and grocery EBIT could fall from 418m EUR toward 350m EUR. The early warning is Kesko's grocery share slipping below 33.0%.

The second risk is slower and more likely: a lower-for-longer Nordic construction cycle. If building permits in Finland and Sweden stay negative year on year into the second half of 2026, the Building and Technical Trade recovery we are paying for simply arrives later, the free cash flow swing weakens, and deleveraging stalls. Watch the permit data before the P&L. On our reading the market prices Kesko for that recovery six to nine months before it shows up in reported profit, so the permits move the stock first.
