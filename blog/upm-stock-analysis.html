UPM Stock Analysis: Our 2026 Fair Value and Rating

Lauri Hynönen · 31 July 2026 · 7 min read

Is UPM stock a buy in 2026?

We rate UPM (UPM.HE) HOLD, not a buy. Our 24 July 2026 report puts the 12-month fair value at EUR 21.10 a share, below both the EUR 22.23 price on the report date and the roughly EUR 23.52 the stock trades at on Nasdaq Helsinki on 31 July 2026. That gap is the whole argument. The recovery case is real, but at today's price the market has already paid for most of it, and then some.

We would not add here. The shares sit about 11% above our target, so on our numbers the balance of risk over the next year tilts down, even though the business itself is in better shape than it was through the 2024 paper downturn. It is a good company trading a little ahead of what our model says it is worth. The shares are not a bargain, and the business is not broken.

What is UPM's fair value and price target?

Our 12-month price target for UPM is EUR 21.10, set in the 24 July 2026 report. It comes from an 8.5x multiple applied to 2027 estimated EBITDA. UPM currently trades near 9.3x that same 2027 EBITDA, against a Nordic forestry peer median around 7.5x. So UPM is already the expensive name in its own sector, and our fair multiple sits between the peer group and where the stock trades.

We grant part of that premium. UPM earns higher margins than most peers, which justifies paying up, though not all the way to 9.3x. The target is sensitive to that one assumption. Move the fair multiple to 9.0x and it lands close to the current price; drop it to 8.0x and fair value falls under EUR 20. That sensitivity is why we treat this as a hold rather than a firm call in either direction.

Why is UPM rated a hold and not a buy?

The recovery case for UPM is genuine, which is exactly why the stock is not cheap. Our model has group EBITDA rising from EUR 1,351 million in 2025 to EUR 1,601 million by 2027, driven mainly by the Paso de los Toros pulp mill in Uruguay reaching its full 2.1 million tonne capacity. Group EBIT margin improves from 7.8% toward 10.2% over the same period. That earnings bridge rests on delivered capacity, not on a hoped-for price spike.

The catch is pulp. We assume hardwood pulp spreads recover toward EUR 122 per tonne, the level our model needs to justify the EUR 5,340 million of enterprise value we allocate to the Fibres division. They sit near EUR 110 today. If Chinese pulp demand stays soft and spreads hold around EUR 110, Fibres is worth less than the current price implies, and the EUR 21.10 target would read as generous rather than cautious. The rating rests on that spread closing, and it has not closed yet.

How do the Sappi JV and plywood demerger change UPM?

Two portfolio moves reshape UPM before the end of 2026, and neither changes our fair value much on its own. The first is a graphic-paper joint venture with Sappi. UPM and Sappi signed a definitive agreement in May 2026 to combine their European graphic-paper businesses into a separate, jointly owned company, and the deal is in an in-depth European Commission review with a decision due by 26 October 2026.

The second is the demerger of UPM's plywood business into a separately listed company, WISA Group Plc. UPM's board approved the plan, shareholders vote at an extraordinary general meeting in early September, completion is set for 31 October 2026, and WISA is expected to start trading on Nasdaq Helsinki around 2 November 2026, with UPM holders receiving one WISA share for each UPM share they own.

Both moves trim the lower-margin, more cyclical parts of the group. We read them as quality-improving rather than value-creating in the near term. We would not pay a higher multiple for the reshaped group until the joint venture actually clears its regulatory review, because a block is a real possibility and the market is already pricing the deal as done.

What would change our view on UPM?

The fastest route from hold to something more positive is a clear turn in the pulp cycle. A recovery in Chinese spot hardwood pulp prices would lift Fibres profitability directly, and Fibres carries the largest single block of UPM's enterprise value in our model. Watch that spread.

On the downside, the risk we take most seriously is a permanent step-up in Nordic wood costs. UPM lost access to cheap Russian roundwood after 2022. If Finnish and Baltic wood prices stay structurally higher, the competitiveness of its Finnish mills erodes in a way no cost programme fully repairs. A European Commission block of the Sappi joint venture is a smaller, more binary risk on top.

There is also an honest risk to the cautious side of our own call. The stock trades about 11% above our EUR 21.10 target as of 31 July 2026, so the market plainly disagrees with us. If the pulp recovery arrives faster than we model, the target is the number that looks wrong, not the price. That is the trade a buyer here is making, and it is a defensible one. We just would not make it at today's quote.
