Best Finnish Dividend Stocks: Yield vs Coverage (2026)

Yield alone is misleading: check payout and free cash flow

Kesko out-yields UPM by 0.6 percentage points and covers that dividend far less comfortably. UPM's free cash flow yield is 10.1% in our 2026 estimate against Kesko's 6.6%. Put plainly, UPM throws off roughly half again as much free cash, relative to market value, as the company paying the bigger dividend.

The payout ratio tells the same story from the earnings side. Our 2026 model has Kesko distributing about 87% of earnings, and its own policy allows 60% to 100% of comparable earnings per share, so 87% leaves little headroom. The closer a payout sits to 100%, the less room a company has to hold the dividend through a bad year without borrowing. That matters more for Kesko than for most, because net debt already runs at 2.1x EBITDA.

Stora Enso is the sharpest illustration on the list. It shows a 4.5% forward yield against a free cash flow yield of just 1.2%. The dividend is a forecast of recovery, not a distribution the current cash generation supports.

UPM has the best-covered yield in our coverage

UPM offers a 4.7% forward yield with the best cash cover on the list, a 10.1% free cash flow yield in our 2026 estimate. On income grounds it is the most comfortable dividend among the Finnish names we model.

The valuation no longer points the same way. We rate UPM HOLD at a EUR 21.10 fair value against a EUR 22.23 share price in our 24 July 2026 report, about 5% of downside. The income case and the value case have separated, which is a change from our previous model. The swing factor is the pulp spread: it sits near EUR 110 per tonne against the EUR 122 per tonne the Fibres enterprise-value allocation needs. If spreads recover to that level the earnings gap closes and the dividend gets easier still. If they stall, the payout leans on a balance sheet running at 1.5x net debt to EBITDA. See the UPM equity report for the full bridge.

Kesko is the highest yield and the fullest payout

Kesko pays the biggest dividend on our Finnish list, a 5.3% forward yield from a consumer-staples grocer rather than a pulp and paper cyclical. We still rate it SELL, at a EUR 17.10 fair value against EUR 19.20 in our 7 July 2026 report, about 11% of downside.

Grocery demand does not swing with the pulp cycle, so the earnings behind this dividend are steadier than UPM's. The trade-off is coverage and price. The payout ratio is about 87% in our 2026 model and the free cash flow yield is 6.6%, both weaker than UPM. On valuation, the market applies one blended 15.8x EV/EBIT multiple across three divisions whose economics differ, and we put the fair blended figure at 14.5x. A 5.3% yield does not compensate for buying the whole group at the grocery division's multiple. Our full workings are in the Kesko equity report.

Stora Enso, Nokia and Wärtsilä: yields we would not chase

The other three Finnish names we cover are not dividends we would buy for income today. Stora Enso shows a 4.5% forward yield, but that figure is a forecast rather than a record, and its 1.2% free cash flow yield is the thinnest cover on the list. We rate the share a BUY at a EUR 13.30 fair value versus EUR 9.17, the largest upside in our Finnish coverage, but the case rests on Consumer Packaging margins recovering from 6.2% toward 10.0% as Oulu ramps. Own it for that recovery, not for the income.

Nokia yields 2.8% and Wärtsilä 1.6%, both too low to anchor an income case, and both carry valuation calls that dominate the dividend. We rate Nokia SELL at EUR 9.40 against EUR 10.28, and Wärtsilä HOLD at EUR 32.10 against EUR 31.89. What international investors keep after Finnish dividend tax

A non-resident individual pays up to 30% Finnish withholding tax on a dividend before it reaches the account, and a tax treaty usually cuts that to 15%. The gross yield is not what a foreign investor keeps. On a 5.3% gross yield, treaty-rate withholding of 15% leaves about 4.5%, while the full 30% domestic rate leaves about 3.7%. Corporate holders face a 20% standard rate.

The number to watch is the nominee-registered rate. When shares are held through a nominee, which is the normal setup for most foreign brokerage accounts, Finland withholds 35% unless the holding is documented for a lower treaty rate. Many brokers apply or reclaim the treaty rate for you, but not all do it automatically. Check how your broker handles Finnish withholding before you buy a Helsinki name for income, because a 35% haircut turns a 5.3% gross yield into roughly 3.4% net. On dividends, the tax wrapper can matter as much as the yield you started from.

Frequently asked questions

What are the highest-yielding Finnish dividend stocks we cover?

Among the Finnish large caps we cover, Kesko leads at a 5.3% forward dividend yield in our July 2026 models, followed by UPM at 4.7% and Stora Enso at 4.5%. Once payout coverage is added we rank UPM first, because its 10.1% free cash flow yield backs the dividend more comfortably than any peer on the list.

Is UPM a good dividend stock?

On income, yes. On valuation, no longer. UPM yields 4.7% in our 2026 model and covers the payout with a 10.1% free cash flow yield, the best cover in our Finnish coverage. But we rate the share HOLD at a EUR 21.10 fair value against EUR 22.23 as of our 24 July 2026 report. The main risk is a prolonged weak pulp cycle that delays the earnings recovery the payout assumes.

How safe is Stora Enso's dividend?

Less certain than the headline yield suggests. Stora Enso paid EUR 0.25 per share for 2025 and currently yields about 2.5%. Our 4.0% forward yield assumes the dividend recovers as the company returns to profit, on a payout near 80% that is above its stated 50%-of-earnings policy. We rate the share a BUY on asset value, not on the dividend.

How much tax do foreign investors pay on Finnish dividends?

A non-resident individual faces up to 30% Finnish withholding tax, usually reduced to 15% under a tax treaty. Shares held through a nominee are withheld at 35% unless documented for a lower rate, and corporate holders face 20%. On a 5.3% gross yield, treaty-rate withholding leaves about 4.2% net.

Do Finnish dividend stocks pay dividends quarterly?

Not usually. Most pay once or twice a year. Kesko is the exception among the names we cover, splitting its EUR 0.90 dividend for 2025 into four instalments across the year. UPM and Stora Enso pay in two instalments, typically around spring and autumn. Quarterly payers are rare on Nasdaq Helsinki.
