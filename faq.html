About the product

An AI equity report is a structured equity research document generated using artificial intelligence and structured financial data. A Valuatum AI equity report starts from today’s share price and shows what it pays for: the price split between the businesses, each business beside its peers, the valuation in familiar measures and the full financial statements. Delivered as a PDF.

Valuatum AI Equity Reports is built by Valuatum Oy, a Helsinki-based financial analysis company founded in 2000. Valuatum has spent over 25 years building equity research and credit risk analysis tools for European financial institutions, stockbrokers, and investment banks. The AI applies that same methodology at scale.

Methodology and accuracy

Reported financial statements and analysts’ consensus forecasts come from Valuatum’s financial model, and the share price and market capitalisation from exchange data. The AI researches the businesses, peers and recent events from published sources such as company releases and filings; it does not change the reported figures or the consensus forecasts. Every fact carries a numbered reference to the source register at the end of the report.

The figures come from reported statements and analysts’ consensus, and the text is checked against them before delivery. The AI can still misread a source, so check the figures you rely on. Every report lists its sources.

The report splits today’s share price between the company’s businesses, so the parts add up to the price after debt and other claims. Each business is valued on a multiple of its forecast operating profit and set beside listed peers. In the Stora Enso report of 29 October 2026, for example, the forests account for €7.57 of the €9.69 share price.

Because the price is the market’s best estimate of what the company is worth, and an AI is not reliably better at forecasting. So the base report shows what each business must deliver for today’s price to hold. An analyst who sees it differently changes those assumptions in a revision, and a 12-month target follows from them. See a real revision →

Enterprise value (EV) represents the full cost of acquiring a business: market cap plus net debt, minus cash. Using market cap alone ignores the capital structure and leads to misleading comparisons between companies with very different leverage. The report reads the price through EV and EV/EBIT, not market cap alone.

Key limitations include: data reflects the report generation date (not real-time); consensus estimates used as forward projections can be wrong; AI can misinterpret or misweight inputs; some sectors like financials, utilities, and real estate require adapted frameworks that may be less precise; and qualitative factors like management quality are hard to quantify. Every report discloses these limitations explicitly.

Reports and delivery

A ready report (€20) has already been written and downloads immediately. Every company page and PDF carries the date it was written, and the figures in it are the ones available on that date. A fresh report (€50) is written for your order today, from today’s data, for a US, Nordic or Baltic listed company, and arrives by email — usually within about 30 minutes. If the company has published nothing since the ready report was written, the two reach much the same conclusions; if it has reported results, guided or moved sharply, only the fresh one accounts for that.
