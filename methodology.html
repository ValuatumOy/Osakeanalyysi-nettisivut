Methodology

How the reports are built.

The report does not invent a value for the company. It starts from today’s share price and shows what each business has to deliver to justify it. A view of its own enters only when an analyst revises it.

01

Start from the share price

The price is the market’s best estimate of what the company is worth, and an AI is not reliably better at forecasting. The report takes it as given and explains it.

  • Share price, market capitalisation and enterprise value on the report date
  • 36 months of weekly prices, and why the price moved in each stretch
  • Analysts’ consensus price target, shown as theirs, not ours
02

Keep the consensus forecast as it is

Analysts’ forecasts of sales and operating profit, held in Valuatum’s financial model, are used unchanged for every year they cover.

  • No forecast is replaced by an AI estimate
  • The model calculates investment, cash flow and the other statements from the forecast
  • History and the analysts’ years only in the financial appendix
03

Split the forecast between the businesses

Analysts forecast the whole company. The report divides the forecast profit between the businesses from the segment results the company reports.

  • Businesses named in plain words, from the company’s reported segments
  • Every figure for one business marked as the report’s own estimate
  • Sales to outside customers kept apart from sales to the company’s other businesses
04

Read the price into the businesses

Each business is set beside listed peers. The price is then split between the businesses so that the parts add up to it, and the report explains why the market pays what it does for each.

  • The measure is EV/EBIT: what the whole company costs, divided by one year’s operating profit
  • A new business that barely sells yet: the profit the price needs from it, and the outcomes and chances around it
  • Where the price meets a documented limit, such as a market’s size, the limit is stated with its source
05

Research with sources

AI researches the company’s recent events, the markets each business sells into and the peers, from published sources. Code decides what is looked up, so every report asks the same questions.

  • A source register with numbered references used across the report
  • Valuatum model output labelled as such
  • Each business’s reported segment figures looked up from the company’s own releases
06

Check before delivery

The text is checked against the figures before the PDF is made. Statements the figures contradict are repaired in place.

  • Every figure in the text checked against the calculation
  • A reader test of whether the report answers its own questions
  • Missing pages and forbidden wording caught by code
07

Show the valuation in familiar measures

The price is also shown the way analysts usually read it, and the calculation from the businesses to the price is printed line by line.

  • EV/EBIT and P/E this year and in the last forecast year
  • Each business beside the listed companies that earn money the same way
  • Economic value added, and the full income statement, balance sheet and cash flow
08

Add a view in a revision

The base report takes no view. An analyst who disagrees writes, in plain language, what should change. The revised forecast runs through Valuatum’s financial model and the report is rebuilt on it.

  • A 12-month target that follows from the analyst’s assumptions; it cannot be typed in
  • Businesses the analyst does not mention keep the market’s view, and their pages say so
  • Reported financial figures never change
