The Remedy revision, line by line ↓

Every report opens by taking the price apart.

The cover splits today’s share price between the businesses: what each is valued at, and on what multiple of its forecast profit. That is the market’s view written down in numbers, and it is what your analyst argues with.

Consensus is kept as it is

Analysts’ forecasts are used unchanged to the last year they cover. The report splits them between the businesses and says which figures are its own estimate.

Each business beside its peers

Listed peers are the yardstick. In the Stora Enso report the mills are priced at about 8.5× 2028 operating profit; their peers at about 11.5×.

No target, no rating

The base report takes no view of its own. A target appears only when an analyst revises the report with their assumptions.

What your analyst can change, and what stays fixed.

An instruction can change

  • A business’s multiple, sales or margin, in the near years or the last forecast year
  • Any variable in the financial model, year by year: tax rate, beta, payout, working capital, investment
  • The size of a market, and the outcomes and chances of a new business
  • How the company is divided: merge two businesses or split one
  • What the text explains and what it keeps short

What stays fixed

  • Reported financial figures
  • The target: it follows from the assumptions and is never typed in
  • Facts need a source: a fact the text lacks is looked up and added to the sources
  • Businesses the analyst does not mention: they keep the market’s view, and their pages say so

What changes for a research team.

The groundwork arrives done

Financials, consensus, the split between businesses, peers and the price read into each business, done the same way for every company. Your analysts start from a finished argument instead of an empty model.

Disagreement becomes a number

Because the base report states what each business must deliver, a view is a specific change to a specific line. When two analysts disagree, you can see exactly where.

The reasoning stays with the report

Each instruction, its change record and every version stay together on the report’s order page. Months later you can see what the market priced, what your analyst changed, and why.

Heads of research

Training that produces something usable

A junior analyst who has to argue with a finished report learns faster than one building a model from an empty sheet, and the instructions show you how they think.

What this does not do.

Worth reading now rather than in week two of a pilot.

Fresh reports cover US, Nordic and Baltic listings

Those are the markets where Valuatum’s statements are kept current automatically. A report on a company listed elsewhere would be built on stale figures, so it is not offered.

Banks and insurers are not what the models are built for

Enterprise value and EV multiples describe an operating business, not a balance-sheet one. Ask before you build a pilot around financials, and we will tell you honestly what a report on one is worth.

It is not investment advice

A base report takes no view. A revised report carries a 12-month target because it values your analyst’s assumptions, but nobody at Valuatum knows your mandate, your holding period or your client. The decision, and the responsibility for it, stays with your analyst.

The research can miss what happened last week

The model reads reported financials, and the AI’s research can be a step behind the news, as the Remedy example shows. A channel check, an unannounced contract or a launch that just happened is the part your analyst adds.
