How payment works, in detail
  1. You set the price. When you publish, you choose what your analysis costs and how long until it decays to free (up to a year). A reader without a membership pays that price by card; a member spends one of their monthly reads instead.
  2. The split is 50/50. Half of what the reader pays is yours, half is ours. The split is taken on the price the reader paid, before card fees — those come out of our half.
  3. Every sale is held for 14 days. A card payment can be disputed or charged back for a while after it happens, and moderation happens after publication rather than before it. For those 14 days your half shows as Held 14 days: counted, but not yet yours to invoice.
  4. Then you invoice us. After the 14 days the money moves to Ready to invoice — that total is what you bill us for, from you or your company. There is a minimum before a payout is worth making, so tiny balances wait for the next sale, and the invoice has a fixed due date rather than an ad-hoc payment date.
  5. A takedown voids the sale. If an analysis is taken down, its sales earn nothing, and anything already paid on it is clawed back against your next invoice. The reader is refunded.
  6. You are not an employee. Payment is against an invoice from you or your company; there is no employment relationship, and any taxes on what you earn are yours to handle.
  7. Member reads are not counted here yet. When a member opens your analysis with one of their monthly reads, nothing is attributed to you today. Only direct sales appear above. That is the next thing to change, and it is the reason a low price and a slow decay to free are worth more than they look: they buy readership, and readership is what the ranking pays for.

This is the plain-English version. The binding one is the analyst terms: ownership, the split, invoicing, takedowns and leaving.
