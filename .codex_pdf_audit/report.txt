UPM Energy
Segment deep dive — Regulated
The market values UPM Energy as a scarce lowcarbon infrastructure asset
rather than a traditional forestry division The valuation is anchored by UPM's
ownership stakes in Finnish energy companies notably Teollisuuden Voima Oyj
TVO which operates the Olkiluoto nuclear plant and Pohjolan Voima Oyj PVO
as well as wholly owned hydropower facilities These assets provide clean highly
flexible baseload power In an era of grid electrification and rising data centre
demand zerocarbon generation with low marginal operating costs commands a
utilitygrade premium decoupling the segment's valuation from the cyclicality of
the wider pulp and paper group
UPM Energy operates fundamentally through the 'Mankala principle' a unique
structural feature of the Finnish energy market Under this model shareholders
provide capital to energy producing companies and in return are entitled to
purchase electricity at cost price in proportion to their holding UPM can either
consume this atcost electricity in its own energyintensive paper and pulp mills
—capturing the margin through lower variable manufacturing costs—or sell the
excess into the Nordic wholesale electricity market Demand is currently
supported by structural electrification cold winter weather patterns and the
secular growth of industrial data centres seeking grid connections in the Nordics
The economic moat is defined by replacement cost and regulatory barriers to
entry Building new nuclear or largescale hydro capacity in Europe requires
decades of permitting and billions in capital expenditure UPM's existing stakes in
TVO and PVO are sunkcost assets that generate power at the bottom of the
regional cost curve When Nordic spot prices spike UPM captures pure operating
leverage When prices fall the Mankala atcost structure still provides a
competitive advantage for UPM's internal industrial footprint
The valuation logic implies that € million in enterprise value is supported
either by € million in recurring EBITDA at a x multiple or by the underlying
asset NAV According to UPM's  Annual Report the Level  fair value of its
energy shareholdings the Mankala stakes is booked at € million
Subtracting this from the € billion allocated EV leaves approximately €
billion in residual enterprise value that must be supported by UPM's directly
owned hydropower assets While hydro assets trade at premium multiples
assigning over € billion in value to the residual hydro fleet suggests the market is
pricing these assets at peak replacement value

UPM Fibres
Segment deep dive — Commodity
The market values UPM Fibres as a highly cyclical commodity platform that
provides substantial operational gearing to global pulp prices The valuation
embeds recovery expectations pricing in the assumption that the segment is
currently operating below midcycle profitability More importantly the market
recognises the structural bifurcation within the asset base the highly competitive
lowcost Uruguayan eucalyptus plantations Fibres South serve as the primary
profit engine offsetting the structurally challenged Nordic softwood operations
Fibres North
The global pulp market is driven by demand for tissue packaging and specialty
papers balanced against large lumpy capacity additions The supply side is
strictly governed by the cost curve Hardwood pulp dominates growth due to its
rapid harvest cycles and lower cost per tonne Softwood pulp is required for
strength but faces structural raw material constraints Following the embargo on
Russian wood imports Finnish operators lost a critical source of cheap pulpwood
and birch triggering a severe permanent upward shift in domestic Nordic wood
costs
UPM's competitive position is entirely dependent on geography Fibres South
operates the Fray Bentos and Paso de los Toros mills in Uruguay supported by
companyowned eucalyptus plantations This creates a firstquartile cost
position globally allowing the segment to generate cash even at the bottom of the
pulp cycle Conversely Fibres North operates in a highly constrained Finnish
wood market In Q  Fibres North recorded a comparable EBIT loss of €
million while Fibres South generated € million in positive EBIT
The allocated enterprise value of € million implies an x multiple on
depressed FY estimated EBITDA € million To justify this valuation on a
standard midcycle pulp multiple of x the segment needs to generate
roughly € million in EBITDA Generating an incremental € million in EBITDA
is mathematically realistic if Fibres North simply returns to historical profitability
The Q  data—where Fibres North recovered to a % EBIT margin on the
back of falling domestic wood costs—supports the feasibility of this margin
recovery
